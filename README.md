# חבורת ההרפתקאות · Puppy Adventures

A gentle learning game for phones, tablets and computers. Original illustrated puppies guide ten-question adventures in Hebrew reading, spoken English, counting, addition, reading an analog clock, and letter recognition and writing. A separate character game practices reading the names of Rubble & Crew. Writing includes English capitals and lowercase, and Hebrew print and handwriting. Each scored adventure has three warm-up steps, four practice steps and three final challenges, within the parent's chosen difficulty bounds. Hebrew uses vowel marks by default. The default spoken-English games require no reading; an optional later path introduces first sounds and word reading.

**Play:** [puppy-adventures.vercel.app](https://puppy-adventures.vercel.app)

## Clock school

The ten chapters are: minutes and hours; numbers around the face; the short hand; the long hand and whole hours; a full turn; half an hour; quarter past; quarter to; counting individual minutes; and building a clock. Concrete minute quantities precede symbolic reading. The hours-to-halves-to-quarters sequence is consistent with the [DfE primary mathematics curriculum](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-mathematics-programmes-of-study/national-curriculum-in-england-mathematics-programmes-of-study) and [NCETM time guidance](https://www.ncetm.org.uk/classroom-resources/cp-year-2-unit-11-time/); the puppy journey and chapter design are this game's implementation choices.

Each chapter starts with an explanation and a model. “בובי מדגים” animates the example and restores the child's clock; a demonstration alone cannot complete a task. The demo and “עכשיו תורי” controls appear above the long explanation. “עכשיו תורי” starts two or more short activities, with optional replays, demonstrations and gentle retries. The puppy journey has large “עוד דקה” and “עוד 5 דקות” controls alongside its minute quantities. Completed chapters receive a check on the map; they do not add fabricated quiz scores or stickers. Stickers continue to come from full ten-question reading games. The first chapter's real-minute activity is optional, can be stopped, and does not substitute a fast animation for actual elapsed time. The accelerated journey is labelled separately.

The shared clock model stores time in minutes. Long-hand dragging accumulates angular travel across twelve in either direction; a full clockwise circuit adds sixty minutes and one hour. The free short hand can also select individual minutes. Both hands always derive from the same value. The hour hand advances half a degree for every minute. One-minute controls support accurate adjustment when a finger is imprecise; dial-number taps remain five-minute shortcuts. The green outer numbers label 5, 10, …, 60 minutes, and the small marks represent individual minutes. Full-turn activities require an actual positive sixty-minute change. Pointer capture, cancellation, large handles, keyboard input and handle-only touch suppression support touch, pen and mouse without disabling ordinary page scrolling.

dist/clock-learning.js defines the curriculum, time arithmetic, bounded lesson flags and saved partial positions. dist/clock-dial.js owns pointer interaction and demonstrations; dist/clock-school.js and its stylesheet render the lesson map, minute journey, real-minute activity and free clock. A completed chapter syncs as one of ten monotone flags through 20261031183138_clock_school_progress.sql. Old clients that omit the field retain completed lessons, and old clock quizzes do not invent teaching history. Current chapter, partial attempt and difficulty remain on that device. The clock stores no drawings or recordings; optional name recordings belong to the separate local child profile described below.

Version 1.12 adds eighty Ava-reference Hebrew recordings, including reviewed feminine minute quantities for all non-quarter positions. Existing 633 recordings retain their hashes. New texts use the same pointed scripts, explicit IPA dictionary, local synthesis and local phonetic diagnostics. The diagnostic check does not prove flawless pronunciation. No paid service or runtime speech request is added.

Browser QA checked a short-hand drag from 12 to 3, a full minute-hand circuit through 3:15 → 3:30 → 3:45 → 4:00, and both hands at finer positions: a long-hand drag to 3:07, a short-hand drag to 4:23 and one keyboard step to 4:24. The opening journey lit one dot for one minute and sixty dots for one hour. A complete real-minute activity ended at sixty elapsed seconds with the clock at 12:01; cancellation also worked. Completed chapters survived reload. The minute-mark lesson completed 2:01, 2:03, 2:05 and 2:10. A full advanced ten-question game awarded one sticker; entering the school for help returned to the same eighth question. A separate return check retained mute. Phone widths of 320 and 390 pixels and an 820-pixel tablet view were checked, including long minute captions and controls without horizontal overflow. These checks do not simulate every physical phone or tablet. Transactional SQL tests cover cross-device lesson unions, offline/in-flight saves, old clients, invalid flags and unchanged execution grants; all generated SQL fixtures roll back.

## Parent settings

Tap **להורים**, then answer the parent puzzle. This is a child-facing navigation gate, not account security.

Choose Hebrew vowel marks, reading word length, math range, English number range, clock level, sound and animation. Under **קריאת שעון**, choose **שעות עגולות**, **שעות וחצאי שעות**, **גם רבעים** or **כל הדקות · דקה אחרי דקה**. The parent view distinguishes questions answered independently from questions that used a hint or another attempt. These are play records, not an assessment. The reset flow asks for confirmation.

Without pairing, progress stays in this browser. To share an album, open **להורים** on the first device, answer **21**, and choose **יצירת אלבום משפחתי**. Use **העתקת קישור למכשיר נוסף** to open a pairing link on the next device. Complete the parent gate there and tap **חיבור לאלבום הקיים**. Alternatively enter the family code manually. Repeat for each device or browser.

Paired devices share completed adventures, skill counts, stickers, completed clock lessons and per-letter practice counters through the free Supabase project. Sound, motion, difficulty and the selected writing letter remain independent on each device. Updates happen after completion, on returning to the page and every 15 seconds while visible. Offline completions survive reopening and upload when the connection returns. Repeated uploads do not duplicate adventures or letter practice. Reset disconnects and clears only that device, preserving the shared album already uploaded.

Expanded sticker IDs are a stable, local catalog in `dist/stickers.js`; rewards derive from the existing shared completion totals, not the legacy twelve-item RPC sticker list. Expanding the sticker catalog itself requires no database migration. Older clients can still upload their counters without removing earned rewards from updated devices. Tests exercise the actual legacy response shape, offline reopening, repeated uploads, and the 1,000th reward across two devices.

The function accepts old clients that omit question counters, retains the original grants, and does not rewrite existing rows. The rolled-back SQL tests cover mixed device versions, stale retries, assistance beyond the old six-answer bound and malformed counters. A 26-sticker legacy album keeps its 156 answers; one new adventure yields 27 stickers and 166 answers across paired devices. `dist/sync.js` also retains ten-question completions made while another upload is in flight.

`dist/letter-progress.js` keeps bounded recognition/writing counters for each alphabet, and the next group prefers unpracticed letters before review. Older versions saved only a combined letter-adventure total. When such a player upgrades, the Hebrew review order begins at ד rather than א, while all historical per-letter counts remain zero; the game does not invent past mastery. New completions record the actual letter IDs. `20261031170249_split_letter_paths.sql` extends the existing code-checked RPC without changing table access or execution grants. Per-device counters merge monotonically, with family totals plus pending offline changes. Recognition updates only on a completed round, writing on a completed trace. Requests send compact counters rather than the 1,000-sticker catalog, staying below the RPC's 4 KB limit. The SQL regression test rolls back all generated test albums, checks old-client uploads and rejects malformed or excessive counters.

Keep the family code private and retain a copy: possession grants access to the album. Pairing links carry the code in their URL fragment, removed on arrival; it is never part of a request URL. ## Audio and offline use

All 880 MP3 recordings are bundled. The 818 Hebrew recordings use the parent-selected Ava sample as a voice reference for the free, local [BlueTTS](https://github.com/maxmelichov/BlueTTS) synthesizer. These are Ava-reference recordings, rather than output from Microsoft's Ava engine. The 62 English recordings use Microsoft's Ava multilingual voice directly. The game does not call a speech service during play or require a speech API key. The optional parent name recorder opens the microphone only after the parent presses its recording button; gameplay does not request it. Hebrew always uses the reviewed recordings; an English browser voice can serve as an English-only fallback. The sound button and replay control let the child start or re-enable audio, including on devices that require a tap before playback.

Each opening attempts the short welcome **שלום אריאל, ברוכה הבאה למשחק שלך!**, respecting the saved sound setting. If autoplay is blocked, the first spoken interaction plays the welcome before that action's instructions. A completed welcome is remembered only for that opening, so ordinary navigation and question changes do not repeat it. The home speaker button can replay the welcome and introduce the puppies. Activity choices, hints, counting, correct answers and retries have spoken feedback; returning to the map invites another adventure. Praise includes **כל הכבוד! הצלחת!**, and retries say **נסי שוב, אריאל. אני כאן איתך.** in feminine Hebrew.

Hebrew narration has a separate, fully pointed source in `dist/speech.js`: instructions address a girl, and counting and clock numbers have explicit feminine readings. `dist/hebrew-pronunciation.js` contains a reviewed dictionary of 668 Hebrew word forms covering the curriculum. It specifies consonants, vowels, stress and feminine endings as IPA phonemes, which BlueTTS uses directly. For example, לִחְצִי is /liχtsˈi/, הַתְּמוּנָה is /hatmunˈa/ and הִצְלַחְתְּ ends in /t/. Silent and audible shva are resolved explicitly across the vocabulary. These phonemes never appear in the child's reading words. A new Hebrew word fails content checks until its pronunciation is reviewed. Device-generated Hebrew speech is disabled so a device cannot reintroduce guessed readings. Slow Hebrew hints make only a small pace change.

A classic service worker caches the game, local fonts and recordings after a successful online visit on HTTPS or localhost. MP3 byte-range responses support Safari's cached-audio requests. The first visit requires a connection. Browser storage policies can remove cached content; revisit online to restore it. The large English speaker button plays the word directly from a tap. Browser layout tests cannot replace a final sound check on the family's physical devices.

## Device support

Use the same HTTPS address in current Safari on iPhone/iPad, Chrome on Android, or Chrome, Edge and Firefox on a laptop. No installation is needed. The layout supports portrait, landscape, small phones, laptop screens, safe areas around notches and home indicators, touch input and keyboard/mouse input. Apple and Android home-screen icons are bundled for optional installation. Sound starts after a tap, respecting browser audio permissions.

Audio generation is a development-only step. Existing recordings need no dependencies. Export the reviewed content first:

```sh
node --input-type=module -e "import {CLIPS} from './dist/content.js'; import {writeFileSync} from 'node:fs'; writeFileSync('clips.json', JSON.stringify(CLIPS));"
```

For English, install the open-source [edge-tts](https://github.com/rany2/edge-tts) client and generate the original Ava recordings:

```sh
python -m pip install edge-tts
python scripts/generate-audio.py clips.json dist/assets/audio
```

For Hebrew, use BlueTTS (tested source revision `712f7c9491ad7875fc985e24e166afe43ae649b0`) and PyAV. Download the [matching v2 ONNX model bundle](https://huggingface.co/notmax123/blue-onnx-v2/tree/cd1093eaa920136e7a727dfb92d5f7879039e587) outside this repository. Pass that directory explicitly; the generator never downloads models. The bundled `scripts/ava-style.json` is the extracted profile of the chosen synthetic Ava sample; no child's voice is involved.

```sh
python scripts/generate-hebrew-audio.py clips.json dist/assets/audio --model-dir /path/to/blue-model --style scripts/ava-style.json
```

The generation environment used NumPy 2.2.6, ONNX Runtime 1.23.2 and PyAV 17.1.0 on CPU. Heavy model files, development packages and source voice previews are excluded from the game and repository. Playback works on ordinary phones and computers without these dependencies.

`dist/assets/audio/manifest.json` records each clip's actual pronunciation input, voice, pace and audio checksum. Hebrew entries also fingerprint the voice profile and model bundle, and record sentence breaks and deterministic takes. Long instructions are synthesized in short sentences. `scripts/hebrew-takes.json` preserves the selected takes so regeneration does not replace a reviewed result with a random variation. A generator retains a recording only when its inputs and checksum match, so changing a shared pronunciation updates every affected recording. Temporary files are replaced only after a valid MP3 is produced. Both generators accept `--keys` for selected clips. `npm run check` rejects missing, stale or mismatched recordings and verifies the Hebrew profile.

`scripts/audit-hebrew-audio.py` checks actual recordings using the local [Hebrew IPA recognizer](https://huggingface.co/notmax123/whisper-heb-ipa/tree/6f7c493615d163a99ed6ea09b2cb7241760d868b). It requires PyTorch, Transformers, NumPy and PyAV, and a separately downloaded model directory. Reports resume only when the audio, expected phonemes, model fingerprint and precision match. No audio is uploaded. The checked-in `scripts/hebrew-audio-audit.json` covers all 818 Hebrew recordings; `npm run check` verifies that its expected phonemes and audio hashes still match. Regenerated recordings require an updated report.

```sh
python scripts/audit-hebrew-audio.py clips.json dist/assets/audio --model-dir /path/to/ipa-model --output scripts/hebrew-audio-audit.json
```

This recognizer is diagnostic: it can confuse sounds, omit glottal stops or misreport stress. Inspect discrepancies and listen to regenerated speech; neither integrity checks nor automated transcription prove perfect pronunciation. Short alphabet names can also confuse this recognizer with written-letter abbreviations or homographs; interpret those outputs with care. The generation services and models may change, but runtime playback uses only the fixed recordings. Run the two audio generators sequentially because they share a manifest.
